# EXW265 Module 2: The Cardiac Gate & Beta-Blocker Logic
## 📘 Module Overview

In this module, we address the "Pharmacological Gate." For individuals with cardiac conditions, common medications like Beta-blockers fundamentally change how the body responds to exercise. You will learn why the standard "Target Heart Rate" (THR) math fails these clients and how to pivot to safe, evidence-based alternatives.

### 🛡️ Tier 1 Safety Gate: The "Beta-Blocker" Redline
**Ref: _SSOT_ENGINE/0.1-master-ssot-library.md**

> [!DANGER]
> **Clinical Rule:** If a client is on ### Beta-Blockers ### (e.g., Atenolol, Metoprolol):
> 1. **INVALIDATE** all Heart Rate (HR) data. 
> 2. **MANDATORY PIVOT:** Use the RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) 6–20 Scale. 
> 3. **RATIONALE:** These drugs cap the heart rate, making intensity calculations based on %HRR dangerous and inaccurate.

### 📖 Core Content: Why the Math Changes
When a client takes a Beta-blocker, their "Redline" is chemically lowered. Using a standard treadmill formula might suggest they reach $140\text{bpm}$, but their heart may be physically unable to exceed $110\text{bpm}$. 
* **The Danger:** Pushing a client to reach a "Math Goal" that their heart cannot meet leads to overexertion and cardiac stress.
* **The Solution:** We use the Borg RPE Scale. We ask the client how they *feel* on a scale of 6–20. For most cardiac clients, a "Solve" of 12–14 (Somewhat Hard) is the target.
